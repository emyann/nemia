A workflow is how a team works on a card: which steps there are, who does each, in what order, what is checked, and where a person
decides. It is a folder of markdown files, and each file is one step. The orchestrator, `packages/agent-orchestrator`, reads it and
runs it.

**It owns** the order of the steps and what each step is allowed: its agent, its tools, its check, its gate, its budget.

**It never touches** whether the work is good. That is a judgement, made in a step by an agent or measured by a check, never by a
condition in the workflow.

**It is reached through** the folder itself. A repository keeps its workflows checked in, one folder each under `workflows/` at its
root, as it keeps its CI; your own sit under `~/.agent-workflows/workflows/` (`WF_ROOT`). Nemia ships no workflow: a factory's are
its own, written by the people who run it.

## A step

A step is one markdown file. Its frontmatter wires it, and its body is its brief: what the step is asked to do.

```md
---
after: implement
agent: reviewer
loop: implement
rounds: 3
budget: 16
---

Review the changes on this branch. Be specific about correctness, not style.
```

The keys are a closed list, and a key the orchestrator does not know is refused by name before anything is spent
(`packages/agent-orchestrator/src/workflow/step-file.ts`). The main ones:

- `after` says which steps must finish first. Steps run in that order.
- `agent` names who does the work: an agent written once, in the `agents` folder beside the workflows (see [Agents](/agents/)).
- `check` is a command that measures the work.
- `gate` makes a person approve before anything after the step starts.
- `loop` and `rounds` send the work back for another round.
- `budget` caps what the step's tries may cost together.
- `workspace` says which folder the step works in: a worktree of the run's own, by default, or the repository's checkout.

The frontmatter is YAML, and every value reads as the text written. Quote a value that holds `: ` or ` #`, or that opens on a sign
YAML reads (such as `[`, `{`, `*`, `&` or `-`), as in `check: "grep -q 'status: done' out.md"`. Write a long or multi-line check as a
literal block, `|-` and the command on the lines below it, indented:

```md
---
after: implement
check: |-
  npm run typecheck &&
  npm test -- --grep "review: findings"
gate: true
---
```

A key that takes a list, such as `after`, reads `plan, design` and `[plan, design]` alike.

The folder's `README.md` is no step: it says what the workflow is for, and the board shows it.

**No key is control flow.** There are no conditions, no expressions and no templating. "Is this good enough?" is not a predicate a
workflow language could evaluate; it is a judgement, and a judgement belongs to a step with an agent in it. The workflow is a team, not
a program.

## The closeout

Every step ends by signing off with a structured answer, its closeout, in a versioned envelope (`apiVersion: "nemia/v1"`): an
`outcome` (`done` or `blocked`), a one-line `summary` for the person who decides, a markdown `body` for the rest, the `artifacts` it
made (files, or named values such as a pull request's address), and what it `needs`, only when blocked. A workflow can ask a step for
more fields with `output`, beside its agent's own, each field declared once, and a later step reads them with `input`, as data. Once
a step signs off, the run keeps its whole closeout as a file named closeout.json in the step's files directory, where the steps after it read it. Claude Code gives the answer through its `StructuredOutput` tool, and Codex through its output
schema. A step that signs off `blocked` stops what waits on it, and says so to the person.

## The check

The run does not take the agent's word that the work is done. A step's check is a command the run executes after the step signs off,
in the step's own workspace.

- Exit 0 is `verified`, and the next step is filed.
- Any other exit is `failed (check)`, and nothing that waits on the step runs.
- A step with no check reads `unverified`, so it never passes for a step somebody checked.

The command comes from the folder as it was when the run started, frozen on the run's record: a check written into the folder later,
even by a step, never reaches the run. Only a check that finished is a verdict on the work. A check killed at its deadline, or stopped,
said nothing about the work, and is run again with `--recheck`.

## Trying again

When a check refuses a step, `retries` says how many times the run sends the work back on its own: it continues the same session and
tells it why. A person can try a step again too, with `nemia retry`: continuing the session with a message, starting fresh, on another
model, or just running its check again.

## A loop and its rounds

A reviewing step can send the work back. Its `loop` names an earlier step to run again, and `rounds` caps how many times, 3 when it
does not say. After each round the reviewer's closeout says `again`, yes or no:

- `again: true`, with rounds left: the next round starts. The earlier step continues its session, and is handed the reviewer's
  findings as data. It answers each finding, fixed or not and why.
- `again: false`: the loop is done, and what waits on it starts.

When the rounds run out, a person decides: one more round (`nemia round`), or enough (`nemia accept`).

## A gate

A gate is a property of a step: `gate: true`. When the step signs off, nothing after it is started until a person approves. The step
reads `awaiting approval`, holds no slot and spends nothing while it waits.

- `nemia approve <run> <step> --message "…"` lets the run go on. The message reaches the next step as data.
- `nemia retry <run> <step> --message "…"` sends the step back with an objection, and it waits at its gate again.

A gate can also be asked for when a run is started (`--gate <step>`), or on a card.

## The landing

The end of a run is a step too, its workflow's last: in a workflow that ships to a branch, that step stages the merge. It runs in the
repository's own checkout (`workspace: main`), has a check, and either has a gate or waits on a step that can send the work back — a
review, whose `loop:` closes on the work.

1. A person approves the gate — or, where the ending has none, the review before it passes, and the run files the step itself. A
   workflow that gives its ending a check and neither is refused when the folder is loaded: its check would commit onto the base branch
   on nobody's word.
2. The step's agent stages the merge of the run's branch onto the base, without committing it, and signs off with the commit message.
3. The run executes the check on the merged tree. On exit 0 it commits the merge; on any other exit it aborts it
   (`landEnding` in `packages/agent-orchestrator/src/ending/ending.verb.ts`, on `landingDue`).

Nothing in the packages knows what shipping means: another workflow's last step might open a pull request, or be a study a person
accepts.

## What a step may spend

`budget` is in dollars: the most a step's tries may cost together, every try and every round. Past it, the step reads `over budget`,
and the run files nothing more of it on its own word. A person raises the cap with `--budget` on `nemia retry` or `nemia round`. The
cost is what the harness priced each attempt at, read by the runtime once the attempt has ended.

## An example

This repository's own team is a workflow, `workflows/build/`: design, then implement, then review looping back on implement, then
land. Its steps are `workflows/build/design.md` (gated), `workflows/build/implement.md` (with a check that runs the whole suite),
`workflows/build/review.md` (`loop: implement`, three rounds) and `workflows/build/land.md` (gated, in the checkout). It is this
repository's, not something Nemia ships.
