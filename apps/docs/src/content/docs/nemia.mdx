Nemia is the name of the whole system, of its command `nemia`, and of the store's built-in agent. This page is about the agent.

She watches every factory in the store for you. She is a long-lived session, woken by the feed. She brings you what is waiting for
you, with a recommendation. She sums up what changed. She turns your answer into a `nemia` command. And she does the rest on her own
judgment, within the level you set. She is not one of the workers: she never writes to a factory's repositories, and never does a
step's work.

**She owns** her session, her memory, and the work you have handed her: watching runs, starting cards, holding a run, and sending a
step a message.

**She never touches** what is yours to decide: a gate, a step's question to you, a loop out of rounds, a step at its budget, and your
levels.

**You reach her** by chatting with her session, from a terminal, the board, or a phone once her bridge is set up. `nemia level` shows
her status and how to reach her.

## Her levels

A level is how much you hand over to her. There are three (`LEVELS`, in `packages/agent-orchestrator/src/level/level.ts`). You set
one for the store and, if you like, one for each factory:

- **`manual`**: she operates nothing. You can still talk to her: a message in her chat starts her, and she rests again after 15
  quiet minutes.
- **`semi-auto`**: she is on. Only what needs you prompts you: answering a gate, a question, a loop, or a cap. So does any write that
  can't be undone, or that changes your settings or a factory. Everything else is hers.
- **`auto`**: like `semi-auto`, with one difference: if your confirmed list allows an answer, given the run's current state, she
  gives it without asking. The built-in rule, "decide when all agree", approves only when all five conditions hold:
  - the step signed off `done` with a recommendation for each question (`recommends`);
  - its check exited 0 (`checked`);
  - no loop asks for another round (`accepted`);
  - no step is over its budget, no proposed cap is above a step's own budget, and the caps don't exceed the run's cost line
    (`withinBudget`);
  - nothing else is waiting for you (`nothingOpen`).

  Anything else still asks you. A rule's `under` keeps its own limit on what the run has spent: the factory's cost line doesn't
  change it.

```bash
nemia level                        # where she is: her level, her session, how to reach her
nemia level semi-auto              # put her on
nemia level --factory pipao auto   # one factory at auto
nemia level manual                 # she operates nothing, and answers her chat
```

You can also raise a factory's level for a period of time. It falls back to its own default afterwards (`Raise`).

## What she is allowed to do

Her limits are enforced, not just written down. Before each of her shell calls, a hook asks for admission through `nemia admit`: a
call admission, run by the runtime as the session's `callAdmission`. It looks up the command's class
(`packages/agent-orchestrator/src/admission/class.ts`) and decides whether the call runs or asks you first. Choosing "don't ask
again" never reduces what waits for you.

On top of that, her agent file (`packages/agent-orchestrator/nemia/agents/nemia.md`) blocks these outright, at every level:

- pushing or merging a branch, force-deleting one, or resetting one with `--hard`;
- opening, merging, or closing a pull request, or creating a release;
- stopping a process by its name;
- running the older `wf` or the runtime's `ae` commands to get around her own commands.

Her instructions also list what she never does: answer a gate on her own judgment beyond your confirmed list, scan the disk for
repositories, raise a level, or edit a run's record, a journal, or a factory's files by hand. When the system is wrong, she says so
and proposes a fix.

Before she acts in a factory, she reads its [instruction file](/factories/#its-instruction).

## The keeper

Nobody starts Nemia by hand. The daemon's keeper does (`packages/agent-orchestrator/src/nemia/keep.verb.ts`). On each beat, it
re-reads the levels, reads her task from the runtime and her chat and subscription from the store, and does whatever is due
(`nemiaDue`):

- While any factory is above `manual`, she operates. Her task is started as a long-lived session. Once it is open, she is subscribed
  to the feed, and she never rests.
- At any level, a message in her chat starts her whenever she isn't running: her task is started, and the message is passed to her.
- Once no factory is above `manual`, her subscription is dropped and she stays for the conversation. After 15 quiet minutes (nothing
  said to her, nothing said by her), she rests: the keeper stops her task. Your next message starts a new task that resumes her last
  session, so her chat reads as one conversation across tasks.
- When your custom instructions for her change, her open session receives the new text through the runtime's `say`, without a
  restart.
- If her session is stopped from outside, it isn't started again until you speak: your next message, or setting a level, starts a
  fresh one.
- On a new store, your first message starts your first conversation with her. You don't need to set a level first.

the runtime say is due. Its state machine is drawn from its code:

How to read it: she is `resting` until you write to her or set a factory above `manual`. Then she is `due` to be started. If the
harness doesn't trust her folder yet, she is `untrusted`, and the keeper grants the trust itself before starting her, in the same
beat: the folder is Nemia's own. She stays there only if the harness's file refuses the write, and then `nemia level`, the board, and
`nemia doctor` explain why and how to fix it. Once `filed` above `manual`, she is subscribed and `kept`. At `manual`, she is
`talking`. When she goes quiet at `manual`, she is `leaving` until her session stops, then `resting`. If her session ends from
outside, she is `stopped` until you speak.

## Her subscription

Nemia is woken by the feed, the log of everything the daemon writes. Her subscription to it is named `nemia` (`NEMIA_SUBSCRIPTION`,
in `packages/agent-orchestrator/src/nemia/nemia.ts`), and only she uses it. Its filter has four selectors (`NEMIA_SELECTORS`):

- `waits=person`: lines that wait for a person.
- `reason=ToolDenied`: a step's tool calls were denied.
- `reason=LevelChanged`: a factory's level changed, from the board, the CLI, or the end of a raise.
- `reason=ConflictsPending`: a factory's upgrade is waiting for a choice on its conflicts.

When a feed line matches one of them, she is notified and takes a look.

This is the orchestrator's subscription to the store's feed. It is different from A2A's subscription to a session's Task, which the
board uses to follow a step live (see [A2A](/a2a/#a-subscription)).

## Her memory, and your words for her

- **Her memory** is one file per fact, in `~/.agent-workflows/nemia/memory/` (`WF_ROOT`). `nemia memory` reads it. Only she writes it.
- **Your custom instructions for her** are in `~/.agent-workflows/custom-instructions/nemia.md`. Edit them in the board's Settings or
  with `nemia agents custom nemia`: your language, your time zone, when you want her to answer for you. They are one of her
  [instruction layers](/agents/#what-an-agent-is-told-five-layers).
