What a session read, by price: input tokens in three classes and the peak context of an attempt — the engine's `Usage`, `ae show`, `wf`, and the board

This is P0.4 of docs/pivot-2026-09-18.md (§1, *Visibility*, and §6). Measured 2026-09-18 over 234 worker sessions: 96.9% of input tokens were cache READS at $0.50 a million, 3.1% cache WRITES at $10, and almost none uncached — a twenty-fold difference in price that the engine adds into one number. `transcriptUsage` (packages/agent-engine/src/runner.ts) sums `input_tokens + cache_creation_input_tokens + cache_read_input_tokens` into `inputTokens`. That sum is what hid a real fault for a week: 43 resumed sessions rewrote 200k–640k tokens each to the cache instead of reading them, and nothing on any screen looked different. The second missing number is how large a session's context GOT: the median worker peaked at 173k tokens and the largest at 831k, and only a script over the transcripts could say so. Since 2026-09-19 a step can cap it (`context: 200k`), and nothing shows whether the cap held.

Read CLAUDE.md, then packages/agent-engine/src/domain.ts (`Usage`, and its rule: every field is a measurement, a count a runner could not see is ABSENT, never 0), src/runner.ts (`transcriptUsage`, `costOf`, how an attempt's lines are bounded by `since` and by the next `agent-name`, and what the context-cap run changed about compaction calls — open issue 26), src/runner-detached.ts (what `claude -p` and `codex exec` report), src/cli.ts (`ae show`, `ae ls`), the engine's README on usage, then packages/agent-workflows/src/run.ts (`Spend`, `stepSpend`, `runSpend`, `costLine`, `runCostLines`, `taskUsage`) and radius.ts, then apps/api/src/app.ts and apps/web/src/lib/api.ts where usage reaches the board, and the pages that draw a step's cost.

What to build:

1. `Usage` gains the three classes as its runner measured them — cache read, cache write, uncached — and the peak context of the attempt: the largest sum of the three on any one response. Propose the names. `inputTokens` stays, and stays their sum, so every journal written before this still folds and every reader of the sum still works; a test folds an old journal. A class a runner cannot see is absent, not 0: say for `claude -p` and for `codex exec` which they report, by measuring, and record it in the engine's README.

2. A resumed attempt's numbers are its own, as `costUsd` already is: the classes and the peak are counted over the attempt's own lines. The peak of a resumed attempt is the context it actually carried, which starts where its parent ended — that is the point of the number, so do not subtract anything from it. A test with a transcript that holds a parent's conversation and a child's.

3. People see it. `ae show` prints the classes and the peak beside the cost. `wf` says them where it says a step's cost (`costLine` and what `wf decide` prints), in a form a person reads at a glance — e.g. `read 21.4M · wrote 0.4M · peak 167k` — and says nothing for an attempt that measured none. The board shows the same on a step's page and on a run's cost, and marks a peak that reached a step's `context:` cap or passed 200k with none. A cache WRITE larger than half the attempt's peak on a resumed attempt is the signature of a rebuilt cache: mark it, in words that say what it means.

4. Nothing is computed from prices here. The engine never turns tokens into dollars (`costUsd` is the harness's own figure); keep it so.
