Nemia is the name of the whole system, of its command `nemia`, and of the store's own agent. This page is about the agent.

She watches every factory of the store for you. She is a long-lived session, woken by the feed. She brings you what waits on you, with a
recommendation; she recaps what moved; she carries your answer back as a `nemia` verb; and she does the rest on her own judgement,
within the level you set. She is not one of the workers: she never writes in a factory's repositories, and never does a step's work.

**She owns** her session, her memory, and the store's work you have handed her: watching runs, starting cards, holding a run, telling a
step something.

**She never touches** what is yours to decide: a gate, a step's question to you, a loop out of rounds, a step at its budget, and your
levels.

**She is reached through** chat with her session, from a terminal, the board, or a phone once her bridge is up. `nemia level` says where
she is and how to reach her.

## Her levels

A level is how much you hand over. There are three (`LEVELS`, in `packages/agent-orchestrator/src/level/level.ts`), set on the store
and, if you like, on each factory:

- **`manual`**: she is off. Nothing runs her.
- **`semi-auto`**: she is on. Only what waits on you prompts you: an answer to a gate, a question, a loop or a cap is asked of you, and
  so is a write that cannot be undone or changes your settings or a factory. The rest is hers.
- **`auto`**: as `semi-auto`, with one difference: an answer your confirmed list allows, in the run's current state, she gives without
  asking. The rule that ships, "decide when all agree", approves only when five conditions all hold: the step signed `done` with a
  recommendation for each question (`recommends`), its check exited 0 (`checked`), no loop asks for another round (`accepted`), no
  step is past its budget (`withinBudget`), and nothing else waits on you (`nothingOpen`). Anything else still asks you.

```bash
nemia level                        # where she is: her level, her session, how to reach her
nemia level semi-auto              # put her on
nemia level --factory pipao auto   # one factory at auto
nemia level manual                 # put her off
```

A factory's level can also be raised for a stretch of time, and falls back to its own default after it (`Raise`).

## What she is allowed to do

Her fence is enforced, not written as advice. Before each of her shell calls, a hook asks her admission, `nemia admit`: a call's
admission, run by the runtime as the session's `callAdmission`. It looks up the command's class
(`packages/agent-orchestrator/src/admission/class.ts`) and decides whether the call runs, or prompts you first. A "don't ask again"
never widens what waits on you.

On top of that, her agent file (`packages/agent-orchestrator/nemia/nemia.md`) denies outright, whatever the level:

- pushing or merging a branch, force-deleting one, or resetting one with `--hard`;
- opening, merging or closing a pull request, or making a release;
- stopping a process by its name;
- running the older `wf` or the runtime's `ae` commands, around her own verbs.

And her instructions say what she never does: answer a gate on her own judgement beyond your confirmed list, scan the disk for
repositories, raise a level, or edit a run's record, a journal or a factory's files by hand. When the machinery is wrong, she says so
and proposes the fix.

Before she acts in a factory, she reads its [instruction](/factories/#its-instruction).

## The keeper

Nobody starts Nemia by hand. The daemon's keeper does (`packages/agent-orchestrator/src/nemia/keep.verb.ts`). On each beat it reads
the levels again, reads her task off the runtime and her subscription off the store, and does what is owed (`nemiaDue`):

- While any factory is above `manual`, her task is filed as a long-lived session, and once it is open she is subscribed to the feed.
- Once none is, her session is stopped and her subscription dropped.
- When your custom instruction for her changes, her open session is told the new words through the runtime's `say`, with no restart.
- A session stopped from outside is never filed again: setting a level above `manual` again starts a fresh one.

The keeper is level-triggered like the tick: it carries nothing between beats, so after a restart it owes exactly what the files and
the runtime say. Its machine is drawn from its code:

Read it this way: she is `off` until a factory is above `manual`, then `due` to be filed. If the harness does not yet trust her folder,
she waits `untrusted` until it does. Once `filed`, she is subscribed and `kept`. A session that settles from outside leaves her
`stopped`. When no factory is above `manual`, she is `leaving` until her session is stopped and her subscription dropped, then `off`.

## Her subscription

Nemia is woken by the feed, the line of everything the daemon writes. Her subscription to it is named `nemia`
(`NEMIA_SUBSCRIPTION`, in `packages/agent-orchestrator/src/nemia/nemia.ts`), and is hers alone. When a feed line matches its wake words,
she is told, and looks.

This is the orchestrator's subscription to the store's feed. It is not A2A's subscription to a session's Task, which the board uses to
follow a step live (see [A2A](/a2a/#a-subscription)).

## Her memory, and your words for her

- **Her memory** is one fact a file, in `~/.agent-workflows/nemia/memory/` (`WF_ROOT`). `nemia memory` reads it. She alone writes it.
- **Your custom instruction for her** is `~/.agent-workflows/custom-instructions/nemia.md`, written on the board's Settings or with
  `nemia agents custom nemia`: your language, your time zone, when you want her to answer for you. It is one of her
  [instruction layers](/agents/#what-an-agent-is-told-five-layers).
